\section{Functional and entropy--transport consequences}\label{sec:consequences}

The geometric theorem has two consequences outside the class of convex bodies. We first prove \cref{cor:functional-mahler}, using the all-dimensional geometric-to-functional implication, and verify its sharp constant. We then give the entropy--transport formulation, including the additional regularity assumption on the densities. Neither argument is an input to the geometric proof.

\subsection{The functional inequality and sharpness}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:functional-mahler}]
Set $f=\ee^{-\varphi}$, a nonzero integrable log-concave function. For $z\in\mathbb{R}^n$, define
\[
 f^z(y)=\inf_{\{x:f(x)>0\}}
        \frac{\ee^{-\langle x-z,y-z\rangle}}{f(x)},
 \qquad
 \mathcal{P}(f)=
 \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f(x)\,\rd x\right)
 \inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f^z(y)\,\rd y.
\]
Fradelizi and Meyer proved that the general geometric Mahler inequality in every dimension implies $\mathcal{P}(f)\ge \ee^n$ for every log-concave function on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with $0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f<\infty$ \cite[Proposition~2(a)]{FradeliziMeyer2008}. \Cref{thm:mahler} supplies precisely this all-dimensional hypothesis. At $z=0$ the defining infimum gives $f^0=\ee^{-\varphi^*}$, so the product in \cref{cor:functional-mahler} is at least $\mathcal{P}(f)$.
\end{proof}

The translation-minimized bound used in the proof controls the origin-centered conjugate without imposing a centering or normalization condition on $\varphi$. The cited implication applies the geometric inequality to auxiliary bodies in dimensions $n+m$ and then lets $m$ tend to infinity; it is not merely a fixed-dimensional reformulation.

For sharpness, let $\iota_A$ be zero on $A$ and $+\infty$ outside $A$. The potential and its transform
\[
 \varphi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i+\iota_{[-1,\infty)^n}(x),
 \qquad
 \varphi^*(y)=n-\sum_{i=1}^n y_i+\iota_{(-\infty,1]^n}(y)
\]
satisfy $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\ee^{-\varphi}=\ee^n$ and $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\ee^{-\varphi^*}=1$. This example attains the constant $\ee^n$. The simplex equality classification in \cref{thm:mahler} does not by itself classify equality for functional Mahler: the limiting implication does not transfer equality cases \cite[p.~1437]{FradeliziMeyer2008}. For even potentials, the symmetric companion gives the sharper constant $4^n$ \cite[Corollary~1.2]{OpenAISymmetricMahler2026}.

\subsection{The entropy--transport inequality}

The functional inequality also has an entropy--transport formulation. For a log-concave probability measure $\eta$ with a density, let $H(\eta)=\int\log(d\eta/dx)\,d\eta$ denote its entropy relative to Lebesgue measure, the negative of differential Shannon entropy. For probability measures $\mu_1,\mu_2$ with finite first moments, set
\[
 \mathcal{T}(\mu_1,\mu_2)=
 \inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left\{\int f\,\rd \mu_1+\int f^*\,\rd \mu_2\right\},
\]
where $\mathcal{F}$ is the class of proper lower-semicontinuous convex functions $\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$ and $f^*$ is the Fenchel--Legendre transform. The cost $\mathcal{T}$ is allowed to be $+\infty$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:entropy-transport}
For every integer $n\ge1$, let $\eta_i(dx)=\ee^{-V_i(x)}\,\rd x$, $i=1,2$, be full-dimensional log-concave probability measures, with proper lower-semicontinuous convex potentials $V_i$. Suppose their densities are essentially continuous, meaning that $\ee^{-V_i}=0$ at $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-almost every point of $\partial\operatorname{supp}\eta_i$. Their moment measures $\nu_i=(\nabla V_i)_\#\eta_i$ have finite first moments, $H(\eta_i)=-\int V_i\,d\eta_i$ is finite, and
\[
 H(\eta_1)+H(\eta_2)\le -3n+\mathcal{T}(\nu_1,\nu_2).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The moment measure is defined using the almost-everywhere gradient on the interior of the support. Lemma~4 and the proof of Proposition~7 of \cite{CorderoErausquinKlartag2015} give the integrability of $|\nabla \ee^{-V_i}|$ there and of $V_i$ against $\eta_i$. Thus the identity $|\nabla \ee^{-V_i}|=|\nabla V_i|\ee^{-V_i}$ gives the finite first moment of $\nu_i$, and the second integrability statement gives the finiteness of $H(\eta_i)$. Gozlan's entropy--transport equivalence \cite[Theorem~1.3 in the author manuscript]{FradeliziGozlanZugmeyer2023} applies to \cref{cor:functional-mahler} with $c=e$. Its constant is $-n\log(e\,\ee^2)=-3n$.
\end{proof}
