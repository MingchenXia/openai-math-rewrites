\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

A convex body is a compact convex subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ with nonempty interior. If $K=-K$, its center $0$ is an interior point, and its polar body is
\[
 K^\circ=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^n:\langle x,y\rangle\le1
                         \text{ for every }x\in K\}.
\]
Write $|K|$ for $n$-dimensional Lebesgue volume and $P(K)=|K|\,|K^\circ|$ for the volume product. The two determinant factors cancel under an invertible linear change of coordinates, so $P(TK)=P(K)$. Mahler's symmetric volume-product problem asks how small this linear invariant can be. The conjectured value is $4^n/n!$, the product of a cube and its polar cross-polytope. We prove this bound in every dimension and classify the equality cases.

The extremal bodies are defined recursively. A centered nondegenerate interval is a one-dimensional \emph{Hanner polytope}. Given Hanner polytopes $H_1\subset\mathbb{R}^k$ and $H_2\subset\mathbb{R}^l$, their Cartesian product and the convex hull
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_1\oplus_\infty H_2&=H_1\times H_2,\\
 H_1\oplus_1 H_2&=
 \conv\bigl((H_1\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times H_2)\bigr)
 \end{aligned}
\]
are Hanner polytopes in $\mathbb{R}^{k+l}$. These are the $\ell_\infty$ and $\ell_1$ sums, respectively. This class contains cubes, cross-polytopes, and mixed recursive sums. We call an invertible linear image of a Hanner polytope a \emph{linear Hanner body}.

\begin{maintheorem}\label{thm:main}
For every integer $n\ge1$ and every origin-symmetric convex body $K\subset\mathbb{R}^n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-inequality}
 |K|\,|K^\circ|\ge\frac{4^n}{n!}.
\end{equation}
Equality holds if and only if $K$ is a linear Hanner body.
\end{maintheorem}

This exposition follows the proof in \cite{OpenAIMathSource}.

Mahler proved the planar inequality \cite{MahlerPlanar1939}. Iriyeh and Shibata proved the result with its equality cases in dimension three \cite[Theorem~1.1]{IriyehShibata}. For unconditional bodies, the sharp bound is due to Saint-Raymond and the equality classification to Meyer and Reisner \cite{SaintRaymond1981,Meyer1986,Reisner1987}. The norm-sum formulas in \cref{sec:convex} give the volume product of the Hanner bodies by induction.

\subsection{The simplex integral}

Start with a body $A=\{X:|b_i\cdot X|\leq1,\ 1\leq i\leq m\}$, with the rows $b_i$ nonzero, pairwise nonproportional and spanning $\mathbb{R}^d$. The lens of \cref{sec:lens} has horizontal half-width $\lambda(t)$ at height $t$. At $X\in\operatorname{int}A$, form the feasible set of $Y$ satisfying $|b_i\cdot Y|\leq\lambda(b_i\cdot X)$ for every $i$. Whenever $d$ independent signed constraints are tight at the same $Y$, the corresponding signed rows and $0$ span a simplex in $A^\circ$. Let $\Sigma_X$ be the union of these simplices.

The conformal map from the disk to the lens has a uniform imaginary boundary coordinate when the angular parameter is uniform. The map comes from Gross's uniform example \cite[Section~3.2]{Gross}. A Stokes estimate applied to high powers of the inverse map gives a lower bound for the sum of feasibility probabilities attached to the independent row sets. The boundary law expresses this sum as
\[
 \frac{d!}{4^d}\int_A |\Sigma_X|\,\rd X\geq1
\]
(\cref{sec:holomorphic-mass,sec:feasibility,sec:simplex}). Since $\Sigma_X\subset A^\circ$, this gives the inequality for $A$. Approximation extends it to every origin-symmetric convex body.

\subsection{Equality}

If $K$ attains the bound, then so does $B=K\oplus_1[-1,1]$. This extra coordinate lets us approach an apex of $B$, where the lens has a prescribed asymptotic width. The integrated missing volume of the simplex unions tends to zero along polytopal approximations of $B^\circ$. \Cref{sec:zero-deficit} turns this into tight-constraint representations at every interior point of $B$, including degenerate limits. At the apex, these representations give a support inequality and then a metric median for each triple in the norm whose unit ball is $K^\circ$ (\cref{sec:median}). The finite-dimensional theorem of Hansen and Lima identifies the resulting norm balls as Hanner bodies \cite[Corollary~7.4]{HansenLima1981}.

\subsection{Functional Mahler inequality}\label{subsec:sec-functional-mahler}

The volume-product problem has a functional form in which polarity is replaced by convex conjugation. For a proper lower-semicontinuous convex function $\varphi:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$, its Fenchel--Legendre transform is
\[
 \varphi^*(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^n}\{\langle x,y\rangle-\varphi(x)\},
 \qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^n.
\]
We use the convention $\ee^{-\infty}=0$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:functional-mahler}
For every integer $n\ge1$ and every even proper lower-semicontinuous convex function $\varphi:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$ such that $0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\ee^{-\varphi(x)}\,\rd x<\infty$,
\[
 \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\ee^{-\varphi(x)}\,\rd x\right)
 \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\ee^{-\varphi^*(y)}\,\rd y\right)\ge4^n.
\]
The second integral is allowed to be infinite, in which case the inequality is immediate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Put $f=\ee^{-\varphi}$. Fradelizi and Meyer's geometric-to-functional implication \cite[Proposition~1(a)]{FradeliziMeyer2008}, applied with \cref{thm:main} in all dimensions, gives
\[
 \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f\,\rd x\right)
 \inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}
 \inf_{f(x)>0}\frac{\ee^{-\langle x-z,y-z\rangle}}{f(x)}\,\rd y\geq4^n.
\]
At $z=0$ the inner infimum is $\ee^{-\varphi^*(y)}$. The choice $\varphi(x)=\sum_{j=1}^n |x_j|$ attains the bound, since its transform is zero on $[-1,1]^n$ and infinite outside, so both integrals are $2^n$.
\end{proof}

The functional inequality also has an entropy--transport formulation. For a log-concave probability measure $\eta$ with a density, let $H(\eta)=\int\log(d\eta/dx)\,d\eta$ denote its entropy relative to Lebesgue measure, the negative of differential Shannon entropy. For probability measures $\mu_1,\mu_2$ with finite first moments, set
\[
 \mathcal{T}(\mu_1,\mu_2)=
 \inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left\{\int f\,\rd \mu_1+\int f^*\,\rd \mu_2\right\},
\]
where $\mathcal{F}$ is the class of proper lower-semicontinuous convex functions $\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$ and $f^*$ is the Fenchel--Legendre transform. The cost $\mathcal{T}$ is allowed to be $+\infty$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:entropy-transport}
For every integer $n\ge1$, let $\eta_i(dx)=\ee^{-V_i(x)}\,\rd x$, $i=1,2$, be full-dimensional log-concave probability measures, both symmetric about the origin, with proper lower-semicontinuous convex potentials $V_i$. Suppose their densities are essentially continuous, meaning that $\ee^{-V_i}=0$ at $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-almost every point of $\partial\operatorname{supp}\eta_i$. Their moment measures $\nu_i=(\nabla V_i)_\#\eta_i$ have finite first moments, $H(\eta_i)=-\int V_i\,d\eta_i$ is finite, and
\[
 H(\eta_1)+H(\eta_2)\le -n\log(4\ee^2)+\mathcal{T}(\nu_1,\nu_2).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $\rho_i=\ee^{-V_i}$, the identity $|\nabla\rho_i|=|\nabla V_i|\ee^{-V_i}$ holds almost everywhere on the interior of the support. Lemma~4 and the proof of Proposition~7 of \cite{CorderoErausquinKlartag2015} give $\int |\nabla\rho_i|<\infty$ and $V_i\in L^1(\eta_i)$, respectively. The pushforward definition of $\nu_i$ therefore gives its finite first moment, and $H(\eta_i)=-\int V_i\,d\eta_i$ is finite. Gozlan's equivalence between the symmetric functional inverse Santal\'o and entropy--transport inequalities, as stated in \cite[Theorem~1.3 in the author manuscript]{FradeliziGozlanZugmeyer2023}, applies to \cref{cor:functional-mahler} with $c=4$.
\end{proof}
